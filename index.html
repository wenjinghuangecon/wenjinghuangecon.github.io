Job Market Paper

Lending Your Way to Currency Dominance: Chinese Overseas Lending and RMB Invoicing

(with Shu Lin and Haichun Ye)    Paper · Slides

This paper examines whether Chinese overseas lending promotes RMB invoicing. Combining loan-level data from AidData with bilateral trade data, we find that greater Chinese loan-to-GDP exposure significantly strengthens RMB exchange-rate pass-through into recipient countries' import prices and import volumes from China, while weakening dollar pass-through. The effects are concentrated in loans extended by Chinese policy banks, non-resource-backed loans, and infrastructure-related products. These patterns are consistent with a procurement-tying mechanism: Chinese official loans raise demand for related Chinese goods, which are more likely to be invoiced in RMB. The results are robust to alternative explanations based on bilateral swap lines, borrower financial distress, political alignment, and currency matching. Placebo tests using World Bank loans and Japanese and U.S. official development assistance confirm that the effects are unique to Chinese official lending.
